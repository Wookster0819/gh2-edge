Retirement, Read Plainly.

Most people feel ready to retire. Most people haven’t checked. We asked — and the surprise wasn’t fear. It was confidence no one had verified.

There’s a story we tell about people approaching retirement: anxious, under-saved, quietly panicking. When we actually asked, the picture came back different — calmer, more self-assured, and in its own way, more worrying. This audience isn’t paralyzed by fear. It’s carrying a confidence that no one has ever pressure-tested.

01

The mood is confident — and remarkably calm

Roughly three in four people described themselves as confident about their retirement. When we asked what worries them most, “nothing worries me much” was nearly the most common answer — running neck and neck with “not knowing if I have enough.” That’s a striking amount of calm for a decision this big.

Is the confidence earned, or just comfortable?

02

The real barrier isn’t cost. It’s trust.

Asked what has actually stopped them from getting help, more people chose “I don’t know whom to trust” than anything else — ahead of cost, and well ahead of “I don’t know where to start.” The most common way people describe a financial professional’s job is “selling to me,” not “helping me.”

What earns trust is unglamorous: a client-first standard, plain-English explanations, clear fees, no pressure. Not magic returns. Just proof.

03

There’s a cliff a lot of people can’t see

About a third of respondents sit below ~$750K — the point where the math for a 30-year retirement gets genuinely tight. The unsettling part isn’t the number. It’s that most of the people below that line still describe themselves as confident. Low balances simply aren’t translating into felt risk.

04

Almost no one can name their number

Ask what balance would let them feel safe, and about one in five can’t name one at all. Many who do name a figure set it above what they currently hold — they sense a shortfall but have no plan to close it.

Here is your number, and here is why. You can’t feel safe against a target you’ve never been given.

05

The deepest fear is outliving your money

The single biggest fear was outliving savings — now ahead of even healthcare costs. But close behind sits a very different worry: spending too little and missing out. People need both permission to enjoy what they saved and the certainty that it will last.

06

They want lifetime income — but the tools are a black box

More than eight in ten said a tool that guarantees lifetime income would be useful. Yet the product built to do exactly that — the annuity — is also the one they understand least, by a wide margin. The appetite exists. The plain-English explanation does not.

So what do we do with this?

The takeaway isn’t “be more afraid.” It’s the opposite: turn quiet confidence into verified confidence. Ask the person guiding your money how you can confirm they’re held to a client-first standard. Get an actual target number instead of a feeling. And make anyone explain the tools to you in plain language before you buy them.
